What happened

NASA Earth Observatory’s Image of the Day for September 18, 2026, by Lindsey Doermann, with images by Lauren Dauphin from Landsat 8, shows Lake Powell at a new record-low surface elevation. On September 10, 2026, the reservoir sat at 3,517.24 feet — below the prior record of 3,519.92 feet set April 13, 2023. That is the Science filing: a dated satellite pair, a measured elevation, and a Colorado River storage story told in canyon walls.

Powell first crossed the prior record on August 15, 2026. The drop tracks a snow drought across the Upper Colorado River Basin in winter 2025–26; Lake Mead also reached a record low in August 2026. Even at the new mark, Powell remains above the minimum power pool of 3,490 feet. The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation took intervention steps in April 2026 as forecasts tightened.
