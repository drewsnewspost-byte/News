What happened

Archaeology Newsroom, dated 9 September 2026, reports that researchers at the University of York and Curtin University used isotopic fingerprinting of microscopic zircon and apatite to match the Devil’s Arrows near Boroughbridge to gritstone at Brimham Rocks — about 11 miles, or 18 kilometers, west. The work settles a long-running debate: the ~25-tonne pillars were not local glacial erratics left by ice sheets, and builders bypassed nearer gritstone sources to haul specific stone from Brimham. The study is published in Proceedings of the Royal Society A. Folklore about a devil hurling stone arrows from How Hill is entertainment garnish only — the science tracks a Neolithic quarry journey.

The team also confirms that a missing Arrow is “hiding in plain sight”: mineral fingerprinting shows the medieval Battle Cross standing in Aldborough is a carved segment of a long-lost Arrow, proving a 19th-century theory by antiquarian J.R. Walbran and older local lore. The three surviving pillars form Britain’s tallest standing-stone row, with heights near seven metres (23 ft).
