What happened

ScienceAlert, dated 28 August 2026, revisits a Curiosity NavCam frame from 20 August 2023 that has been recirculating as a bizarre jellyfish silhouette on the Martian limb. The dark smudge looks a little like tentacles or a War of the Worlds tripode, but companion frames taken 13 and 25 seconds earlier of the same scene show no hint of it. That single-frame behavior is the tell.

The leading explanation is a charged particle from space striking Curiosity silicon detector: Mars lacks Earth thick atmosphere and global magnetic shield, so cosmic-ray artifacts routinely pepper rover images as bright spots, streaks, or occasional dark glitches. Imaging scientist Justin Maki has noted bright spots nearly every week in Curiosity imagery. Without a formal Curiosity-team write-up of this exact frame, other camera artifacts cannot be fully ruled out, but the images make one thing clear: whatever made the jellyfish was almost certainly happening in the camera, not floating on the ridge.
