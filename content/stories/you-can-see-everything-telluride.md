What happened

The Los Angeles Times, dated 7 September 2026, reports that Telluride Film Festival’s mystery Sunday slot was Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s documentary You Can See Everything, a nearly three-hour A24 project about convicted Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, filmed largely in secret over three years. Festival director Julie Huntsinger had teased “very known names” and a jaw-on-the-floor length. Speculation ran through Aaron Sorkin’s The Social Reckoning, Denis Villeneuve’s Dune: Part Three, and Alex Gibney’s Musk before the reveal. Hundreds of moviegoers locked phones in pouches before entering the Werner Herzog Theatre; security with night-vision goggles watched for anyone who kept a device free. Huntsinger’s stage line was the festival classic: what happens in Telluride stays in Telluride.

Fielder, joined by Oppenheim, opened with a deadpan apology for anyone disappointed by who walked out. The movie begins 34 days before Holmes is due to report to federal prison, with the crew given extraordinary access to her life with partner Billy Evans and their young children. Holmes and Evans pitch “radical transparency.” Anyone who knows Fielder’s work can guess the rest: the filmmaker is pulled into the frame, reenactments scramble reality and performance, and a Hollywood actress’s arrival is treated as too good to spoil. Emma Stone moderated the post-screening Q&A. A24 will release the film theatrically in October. Neither Holmes nor Evans has seen the finished cut, per the Times.
