## What happened

NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is “go” for launch. The agency and SpaceX finished the Launch Readiness Review on Friday at Kennedy Space Center, the last gate before countdown, NASA’s Roman blog says. That review is the moment the rocket, the observatory, the weather shop, and the people on console all have to be ready at once. Liftoff is targeted for 7:26 a.m. EDT Sunday, Aug. 30, from Launch Complex 39A. Space Launch Delta 45 weather officers put favorable conditions at 60 percent.

It would be NASA’s sixth primary Falcon mission from Kennedy, after IXPE, Psyche, GOES-U, Europa Clipper, and IMAP. Roman is built for deep, sweeping surveys: dark energy and dark matter, planets around other stars, maps of billions of galaxies, black holes, and a public data flood. SpaceNews, covering the same pad, notes a 300-megapixel infrared camera, a coronagraph technology demonstration, a $4.3 billion budget, and a five-year prime mission headed for the Earth-sun L2 point, about 1.5 million kilometers out.

## Why it matters

Hubble and Webb take exquisite small patches of sky. Roman is the wide shot. Scientific American, writing ahead of the same launch, said the mirror can see about 100 times more sky in one exposure than Hubble or Webb, which is how you count galaxies and stars in bulk instead of one postcard at a time. That also means a data problem: NASA has said Roman may produce on the order of 1.4 terabytes of science data a day. The point of “go” on Friday is that the hardware meant to feed that firehose is cleared to leave the ground.

If the clock holds, the observatory takes roughly 100 days to reach L2, commissioning on the way. Science starts after arrival. An early launch relative to a formal May 2027 readiness date, as SpaceNews reports, is not trivia. It is months of survey time that do not have to wait.

## Conclusion

Sunday morning is a weather percentage and a countdown, not a discovery yet. The discovery machine is what got the stamp. If it leaves the pad, the sky survey is the story that follows.
