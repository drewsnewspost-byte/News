What happened

NASA Science, dated September 8, 2026, reports that scientists used NASA’s Hubble and James Webb Space Telescopes together to study Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs) — small, faint, icy bodies orbiting beyond Neptune — and analyzed 27 newly discovered tiny, dim TNOs in two complementary papers published in The Astronomical Journal. Teams unexpectedly found fewer small TNOs than some planet-formation models predicted, and the colors of these bodies followed the same relationships as their larger family members — a “remember the past” result that suggests the smallest objects somehow preserve how they were made.

In the deepest TNO survey to date, teams led by PhD candidates from the University of Victoria (under National Research Council of Canada guidance) and Northern Arizona University examined a patch of sky simultaneously with Hubble in visible light and Webb in infrared, measuring colors, sizes, and orbits for both dynamically “cold” and “hot” TNO populations. Webb discovered 27 new, remarkably dim TNOs; one is so faint it is compared to standing on Earth and seeing a small swarm of fireflies on the Moon. The smallest observed has a diameter of about 3 miles (5 kilometers).
