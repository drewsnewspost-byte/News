What happened

NASA’s Hubble mission team describes Cloud-9 as the first confirmed detection of a Reionization-Limited H I Cloud, or RELHIC: a starless, gas-rich, dark-matter-dominated cloud treated as a relic of early galaxy formation. Results appear in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. Principal investigator Alejandro Benitez-Llambay of Milano-Bicocca University frames it as a failed galaxy — seeing no stars is what proves the theory. Lead author Gagandeep Anand of STScI says ground telescopes could not settle the star question; Hubble’s Advanced Camera for Surveys could.

Cloud-9 sits about 14 million light-years away near the spiral galaxy Messier 94. Radio surveys with FAST, later confirmed by the Green Bank Telescope and the Very Large Array, found the gas first. The neutral-hydrogen core is roughly 4,900 light-years across and about one million solar masses of hydrogen; balancing arguments put the dark matter near five billion solar masses. Hubble found no stars within the cloud — background galaxies only. This desk files it as pop unexplained nature: a phantom building block, not a cover-up story.
