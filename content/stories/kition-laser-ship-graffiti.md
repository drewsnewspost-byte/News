What happened

Arkeonews, summarizing a 2026 International Journal of Nautical Archaeology study by Graham C. Braun, Caroline Barnes, and Tzveta V. Manolova, reports that terrestrial laser scanning and photogrammetry at Kition Kathari’s Temple 1 in Cyprus revealed 23 previously unrecognized potential ship graffiti on the southern façade. Nine scored highest confidence, six moderate, eight uncertain — on top of 19 ships published in 1985 plus two on a Temple 4 altar. The ships sort into six morphological groups spanning merchant vessels, galleys, and hybrids with Levantine, Aegean, and local Cypriot parallels. A repair block (T1.8) installed roughly 150 years after the original orthostats also carries ships, so the wall was not one Bronze Age snapshot — carvers kept adding maritime images into the Early Iron Age. Earlier readings treated the graffiti as a single Late Bronze Age fleet; the new chronology makes the façade a multi-generation maritime archive.
