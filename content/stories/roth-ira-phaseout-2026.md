What happened

The IRS newsroom summary of 2026 retirement COLA changes lifts the income phase-out range for Roth IRA contributions to between $153,000 and $168,000 for singles and heads of household, up from between $150,000 and $165,000 for 2025. For married couples filing jointly, the range rises to between $242,000 and $252,000, up from between $236,000 and $246,000. For a married individual filing a separate return who contributes to a Roth IRA, the phase-out is not subject to an annual COLA and remains between $0 and $10,000. Those are published AGI rails for who can still put new dollars into a Roth under the 2026 tables — not targets and not advice to contribute anything.

The same notice also lists other 2026 lids this desk has filed on other days — 401(k) elective deferrals at $24,500, IRA contributions at $7,500, Saver’s Credit AGI ceilings — but today’s jar is only the Roth contribution phase-outs. Notice 2025-67 carries the detailed tables; the newsroom page is the source URL for this package. Educational finance only. No tickers. No “max this.” Ceilings, not a recommendation.

Why it matters

A phase-out range is a different animal from a contribution limit. Prior days filed Saver’s Credit, SIMPLE, SEP, and 401(k) numbers. Conclusion
