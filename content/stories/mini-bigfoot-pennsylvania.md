What happened

PennLive’s J.D. Prose, writing Aug. 25, 2026, passed along two fresh southwest Pennsylvania creature reports that well-known regional researcher Stan Gordon had collected in the same 24-hour window. They are sightings, not specimens. Nobody in either account produced a photograph. The paper treats them as witness statements about shorter Bigfoot-like figures, and that is how this desk files them: as folklore notes from Indiana County and Westmoreland County, sitting in the unexplained drawer until something sharper than a dusk silhouette shows up. Gordon’s name is the through-line. The two counties are the map. The missing pictures are the honest gap.

The first account is dated Aug. 18, on a farm in Indiana County. A woman said she was finishing evening chores when she heard a commotion near the tree line, stepped outside, and spotted a figure crouching behind trees about sixty feet away, then a second figure crouched nearby. Both, she told Gordon, stood about three and a half feet tall, walked upright, and then jumped and ran around as if they were playing. She described straggly, medium-length, light brown hair, darker brown faces, round dark-brown eyes, no neck she could see, and slender arms that reminded her of a child’s. Foliage hid their legs. The noises, she said, sounded like a baby cooing. When she sneezed, the pair stopped and looked at her; she ran into the house. Her husband did not see the figures, though he told Gordon she came in yelling, that their dog later refused to go outside, and that earlier that day, walking the dog, he had heard wood-knocking by a cornfield and then three knocks from a ridge. She also said a similar cooing woke her one night after the sighting.

The next evening, Aug. 19, a driver in Westmoreland County reported a different silhouette. Around 8:05 p.m., on a rural road near sunset, she said a hair-covered creature about five feet tall ran up a hillside toward the pavement and came within roughly thirty feet of her vehicle. PennLive, drawing on Gordon’s notes, describes charcoal grey hair, a dome-shaped head, no visible neck, long arms, a flat nose, and small dark eyes. OutKick’s follow, sourcing the same Gordon file, adds flared nostrils, a body that turned in a way she called almost mechanical, and one hair-covered leg seen clearly as the figure spun and ran back down the hill. Still no picture. Still one witness, one rural road, one hillside.

Why it matters

Southwest Pennsylvania already keeps a deep amateur file of Bigfoot reports, which is why Gordon’s desk is a magnet for this kind of call. PennLive and OutKick both underline that history without turning either August night into a confirmed animal. OutKick is explicit that nothing in the new statements connects cleanly to older regional cases, and that the mystery will likely stay a mystery: no tracks in these two write-ups, no hair sample, not even the usual blurry phone frame. That is the entertainment lane. Short, playful farm figures one night and a charcoal runner the next make a tidy two-county folklore pair, the sort of pint-sized chapter cryptozoology fans pass around because it is weird and local, not because a lab has signed off. This filing stays in that lane: reports, not proof; cryptid scrapbook, not a wanted poster.

The cartoon on this filing stays cute on purpose. Panel one is dusk on the Indiana County farm, two short hairy cartoon stand-ins tumbling near the trees like overgrown garden gnomes who skipped the statue stage. Panel two is the Westmoreland rural road, a five-foot charcoal-grey cartoon figure scrambling up the hill in the last light. Panel three is a close-up of the dome head and the missing neck, still a cartoon, still more storybook than creature-feature. Panel four is a folklore file cabinet stamped UNCONFIRMED, which is the honest label for a week with two stories and zero photographs. No real faces. No gore. Just the shape the witnesses described, drawn as folklore.

Conclusion

What we can say, citing PennLive’s Aug. 25 package and the Gordon accounts it relays, is narrow: two separate witnesses in two neighboring counties, Aug. 18 and Aug. 19, described shorter upright hair-covered figures, one pair at about three and a half feet and at play, one solo runner at about five feet in charcoal grey. Researcher Stan Gordon collected the statements. The region has a history of Bigfoot reports. These two did not come with pictures. This desk is not here to prove a species. It is here to log the tale the way a county historical society logs a ghost story — as an unconfirmed, pint-sized entry in Pennsylvania’s already crowded cryptid scrapbook.
