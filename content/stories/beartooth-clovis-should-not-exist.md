What happened

Mountain Journal, dated 6–7 September 2026, reports that archaeologist Scott Dersam and his Alpine Ecosystems Research Institute crew, working high in the Beartooth Mountains a few miles outside Yellowstone’s border, uncovered what Dersam calls a smoking-gun Clovis find: a Clovis point base sitting in the middle of an ancient hearth, still in undisturbed subsurface context. Prevailing paleoecology and glaciology say that basin should have been under massive Pinedale ice about 13,000 years ago — the era of Clovis people — which is why Dersam keeps repeating that the sediment, the ecosystem, and the habitation “should not exist.” Soil tests he cites show sun-exposed dirt dated to windows when ice maps said the place was sealed. Over four years he has logged six high-elevation Clovis sites, five in the Beartooths; until now, Montana’s only noncontroversial Clovis site was Anzick at lower ground.

The summer 2026 hearth moment was captured on a phone video: brush, then a dental-tool pick, then “Yep, that’s a Clovis base. In the middle of the goddamn hearth.” Students gasped. They had been digging for dirt data, not artifacts. Dersam’s line afterward was lottery-and-lightning twice in one day — a career find that would turn sixty years of high-elevation Paleoamerican assumptions on their head if lab dating holds. He leaves most artifacts in place, removes only pieces walkers might pocket, and frames the work as reconstructing mountain use rather than filling display cases. Peer review and publication are still ahead. This desk is filing the alpine mystery as pop-history archaeology and unexplained science-adjacent curiosity: odd dirt, odd ice maps, odd stone in a hearth. No politics. No identity fights. No modern conspiracy boards.
