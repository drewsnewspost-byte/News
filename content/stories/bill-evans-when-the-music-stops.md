Grant Gee’s narrative feature Everybody Digs Bill Evans sits in 1961, after the bass chair went empty, and treats silence as the subject.

The hits are not the point. A few bars of Waltz for Debby can show up. The empty stool does more work.

Gee is making a film about a pianist by leaving space where the trio used to be.

That is the beat. When the music stops, the movie starts listening.
