What happened

The Debrief reports that Turkey’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism announced a unique stone sculpture at Karahan Tepe, a Neolithic site about 30 kilometers from Göbekli Tepe in southeastern Turkey. Officials call it a composite sculpture: a human figure riding a leopard, about 70 centimeters tall, described as roughly 11,000 years old and a style not previously encountered at the site. Culture and Tourism Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy said the piece was found during ongoing excavations this year, resting on a bench along the wall of a circular structure about three meters across. The floor was paved with flat stones, which the ministry reads as deliberate architectural planning in an era before cities and writing.

Karahan Tepe sits in the larger Taş Tepeler “Stone Hills” cluster. Göbekli Tepe’s T-shaped pillars and animal carvings, dated between about 11,500 and 12,000 years before present, already pushed monumental art earlier than older textbooks expected. Karahan Tepe excavations began in 2019 and are cast as a significant parallel site. The ministry’s announcement, as carried by The Debrief, frames the leopard rider as opening new questions about the earliest periods of human history and about the transition from mobile hunter-gatherer life toward settled communities.
