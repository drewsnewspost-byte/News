What happened

Archaeology Magazine’s Sept. 14, 2026 news brief reports that a marble slab uncovered near the amphitheater at Hadrian’s Villa in Tivoli, Italy, bears the inscription “FVLGVR SVBMANIVM,” linked to Summanus — the Roman god of nocturnal lightning — according to coverage citing The Independent and Italy’s Ministry of Culture. The slab is thought to mark the practice of burying objects struck by lightning, which Romans considered consecrated by the gods: the fulgur conditum ritual. Places struck by lightning were marked as religious spaces where the divine had entered human space.

Fabio Giorgio Cavallero of the University of Umbria said the find opens new perspectives on this sector of Villa Adriana and on the role of fulgur conditum in reorganizing spaces. Alberto Samonà of Italy’s Ministry of Culture called the material sign of sanctifying a lightning-struck place a chance to touch meticulous ritual procedures of the time.
