What happened

Archaeology Magazine’s Sept. 15, 2026 news brief reports that excavations at Troy have uncovered the ancient city’s earliest agora — a central marketplace located in front of the city walls associated with Troy VI, the settlement and epoch typically linked with the Trojan War story. The work was carried out through a collaboration between the Turkish Ministry of Culture and Tourism and Çanakkale Onsekiz Mart University. Coverage cites Hürriyet Daily News on the find.

During the Late Bronze Age the city, then known as Wilusa, collapsed, but the new evidence indicates the site was not vacant for very long. Roughly 80 to 90 years later, new immigrants moved in, building houses atop the ruins and around the previous settlement’s central square, and named their city Ilios or Ilion. Reyhan Körpe, deputy head of the Troy excavations, said the team believes a Late Bronze Age marketplace — an agora — stood there and was revived by those later settlers, making the excavated square Troy’s oldest agora.
