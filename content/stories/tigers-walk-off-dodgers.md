What happened

The Detroit Tigers beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 2-1 at Comerica Park on Saturday because the ninth inning belonged to Javier Báez and then to Kevin McGonigle. CBS Sports, running the Associated Press recap, says Báez made the huge plays in the field and at the plate that set up the walk-off. Los Angeles had the go-ahead run in its pocket with two outs in the top of the ninth when Teoscar Hernández drove a ball to the deepest part of the park. Báez, playing left, sprinted over, called it, and ran it down. That catch kept the game tied. He then led off the bottom of the inning with a double, took third on Max Clark’s sacrifice bunt, and watched Jack Dreyer (5-2) load the bases: an intentional walk to Gleyber Torres, then an unintentional walk to Hao-Yu Lee.

McGonigle, who had been 0-for-16, hit a deep fly to left-center that struck the warning track and bounced into the Dodgers bullpen. Because the winning run was already on third, the official scoring was a single, not a ground-rule double. That is the whole walk-off in one sentence, and it is the part of the box score people will remember. The starters never got the decision. Blake Snell, in his fourth start back, allowed one run on three hits in five innings and struck out 10. Keider Montero gave up one run in 5 1/3. Detroit had taken a 1-0 lead in the third when Báez walked, moved up on Max Muncy’s misplay of Clark’s bunt, and scored on Torres’s double. McGonigle later walked to load the bases with one out, and Snell struck out Dillon Dingler and Eduardo Valencia to leave them there. Freddie Freeman’s RBI single in the sixth tied it after Alek Thomas doubled and Shohei Ohtani grounded out. Tyler Holton then got Tommy Edman to bounce into a 6-4-3 double play with the bases loaded. The game stayed 1-1 until Báez’s catch and McGonigle’s bounce.

Why it matters

This was a one-run game in a three-game series, and the Dodgers have been living in that margin all week. CBS notes the clubs finish Sunday afternoon, with Tigers left-hander Framber Valdez (8-9, 4.35) scheduled and Los Angeles still to say whether Ohtani (8-2, 1.79) will throw an inning, which would be his first mound work since July 3. A walk-off of this shape is not just a W. It is a leftover from every wasted chance: Snell stranding the bases in the third, Holton escaping in the sixth, Hernández’s drive dying in Báez’s glove instead of clearing the wall. The Tigers did not outslug anyone. They got one run on a Torres double, one on a ball that bounced over a fence, and the defensive play that made the second run possible.

McGonigle’s single is also a scoring-rule joke that baseball still takes seriously. A ball that hops the wall is a double on any other inning. In a walk-off with the winner on third, the batter only gets the bases he needs, so the book writes single. That is why the comic’s third panel is a bounce into the bullpen and not a home-run trot. Detroit snapped a two-game slide and kept the rubber game alive. Los Angeles left Detroit with another one-run loss on a night Snell punched out ten and still did not get a chance to win it. The series is not a pennant. It is August baseball in which a left fielder’s running catch and a shortstop’s 0-for-16 hangover decide who sleeps on 2-1.

Conclusion

Báez caught the ball that would have put Los Angeles in front, doubled to start the bottom of the ninth, and watched McGonigle’s drive hop the wall with the bases full. Snell’s ten strikeouts and Montero’s one run did not make the decision. The strip is the catch at the track, the leadoff double, the bounce into the bullpen scored as a single, and DET 2 LAD 1 on a Saturday night in Detroit. Sunday is the rubber game. Saturday is already in the book.
