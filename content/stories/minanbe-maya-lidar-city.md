What happened

Smithsonian Magazine’s smart-news write-up of an INAH-backed expedition describes Minanbé — Yucatec Mayan for roughly “there is no road” — a previously unlooted Maya city found via aerial LiDAR inside Mexico’s Calakmul Biosphere Reserve in Campeche. Excavation leader Ivan Šprajc notes no old logging paths lead there; the team cleared miles of trail before walking kilometers of jungle. After a 2023 LiDAR scan of about 40 acres, ground crews mapped urban plazas, palaces, religious buildings, terraces, and water channels. That is the Conspiracy Theories desk in entertainment/archaeology mode: a hidden city in the canopy, not modern intrigue.

The tallest building is a Río Bec-style temple pyramid more than 40 feet tall. Researchers recorded 14 stelae and altars; epigrapher Octavio Esparza Olguín reads one stela with a date equivalent to 849 CE — near the Lowland Maya collapse window — and another altar with a late-seventh-century calendrical element as the oldest monument on site.
