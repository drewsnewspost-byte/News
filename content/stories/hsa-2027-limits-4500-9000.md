What happened

Finance Pulse’s Saturday-useful explainer separates what is already locked for 2027 from what is still a forecast. Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2026-24, Health Savings Account contribution limits are official: $4,500 for self-only HSA-qualified HDHP coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, up from $4,400 and $8,750 in 2026. The age-55-plus HSA catch-up stays $1,000. That is the Finance filing: a clean personal-finance scoreboard you can actually use for open-enrollment math — educational only, not advice.

The same piece notes that 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b), and IRA limits for 2027 are not official yet. Milliman’s mid-August forecast (after July CPI) still projects a $25,500 employee deferral limit, an $8,500 regular age-50-plus catch-up, and an $11,750 higher catch-up for ages 60–63, but those numbers can still move with August and September inflation. September CPI is due October 14, 2026; the IRS typically publishes retirement cost-of-living adjustments in October or November. Readers also get the 2027 HDHP gatekeeping numbers that determine whether a plan even qualifies for HSA contributions: minimum deductible $1,750 self-only / $3,500 family, and maximum out-of-pocket $8,700 / $17,400. Employer HSA deposits count toward the same annual ceiling. A contribution limit is a ceiling, not a recommendation — and forecasts are not payroll settings.

Why it matters

Open-enrollment season loves a number you can trust. Official HSA caps let households and benefits teams budget now; treating a Milliman 401(k) projection as a January election is how people overshoot or undershoot a match.
