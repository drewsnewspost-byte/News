What happened

PetaPixel, dated 9 September 2026, recounts how a NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory visualization called a decorrelation stretch — built to tease color variation out of Mars rover and satellite scenes — inspired mathematician and rock-art enthusiast Jon Harman to build the Dstretch plug-in. After seeing Mars before-and-after frames at a 2005 rock-art conference, Harman dug up a NASA paper on the algorithm and ported the idea into open-source image tools, later apps. Applying it to Cave of San Borjitas imagery from Baja California pulled a yellow figure out of near-invisibility — the moment he knew the method was useful for faded Earth art.

Between 2010 and 2012, Singaporean archaeologist Noel Hidalgo Tan used Dstretch around Cambodia’s Angkor Wat complex and documented more than 200 faded paintings almost invisible to the naked eye. The tool has since turned up undocumented figures from Egypt to Canada to Greece and even enhanced tattoos on mummified remains.
