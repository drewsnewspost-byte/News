What happened

NASA’s Ashley Balzer, dated September 15, 2026, reports that the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has activated its primary science camera — the Wide Field Instrument, a 300-megapixel infrared imager — while still en route to Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 2. The mission also began initial checkout of the Coronagraph Instrument after waking it earlier this month. That is the Science filing: a dated instrument-activation stamp, not a first-light science gallery yet.

The Wide Field Instrument rested about 10 days after launch-era procedures; on Sept. 11 teams turned heaters off so the instrument could cool toward roughly −225°F before activating its 18 infrared detectors, then ran calibration, exercised the element wheel, and worked the focus mechanism over the weekend as detectors headed toward about −300°F. The first starlight test image shows out-of-focus “donut” stars because the detector array is still stowed from launch; first science images are expected in early 2027. Separately, the Coronagraph Commanding Center at Caltech/IPAC confirmed communication, with thermal conditions near 72°F ahead of a planned 30-day decontamination period. Coverage quotes Josh Schlieder, Wide Field Instrument scientist at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center, and Eric Cady of JPL. Bright Roman gold and deep-space indigo. White gutters.
