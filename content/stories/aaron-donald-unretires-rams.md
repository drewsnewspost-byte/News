What happened

Aaron Donald was on a Los Angeles Rams practice field on Sunday because the retirement that started in March 2024 is over. After ten NFL seasons he had stepped away. On Sunday he agreed to a one-year, $20 million contract with playoff incentives that can raise the total to $30 million, sources told ESPN’s Adam Schefter and NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport. That is the roster transaction. The 35-year-old defensive tackle spent his entire career with the club that drafted him 13th overall in 2014, and the strip’s first panel is the hammock coming down, not a mystery about where he would sign.

Coach Sean McVay said the staff has not decided when Donald will play in a game. The Rams open the season against the San Francisco 49ers in Melbourne, Australia, in Week 1. McVay said the club will take it “one day at a time” as they determine “whether or not we think it’s best to be able to play him in Australia.” The first question McVay asked about Week 1 availability was whether Donald even wants to play. If the answer is yes, the next question is the ramp-up: individual work versus team settings, taking on double-teams, the physicality the position actually requires. McVay said they will feel that out one day at a time and stay in the moment. Donald called the decision “a long process,” one that began 13 to 14 weeks ago. During camp, McVay said the Rams’ June trade for reigning Defensive Player of the Year Myles Garrett had spurred conversation about a possible return. Donald’s own telling was that he watched the pieces arriving, thought “that’s a complete team,” and then thought, “I wish I was still out there playing right about now.”

The training was not a rumor. Donald said it was “13, 14 weeks of just nonstop grinding” to get to Sunday. “It really wasn’t an easy decision,” he said. “It took a lot of hard work, a lot of long conversations with a lot of different people but felt good enough to make the decision to come back and do this.” He said he had been “on and off for a while,” then knew for about two weeks that he wanted to return, and still refused to rush. “Just wanted to take my time and make the right decision because once you choose to come back, you all the way back in,” Donald said. “Can't be halfway in, got to be all the way in it. So, had to get to that point where I felt like I will feel like I'm 100% committed to playing football again because the last two years been amazing.” That last clause is the whole tone of the deal: he is not dabbling. The contract is one year. The commitment line is the one ESPN put in the headline.

Why it matters

This is a star interior rusher rejoining the only NFL locker room he has ever had, on a short-term sheet, with a Week 1 trip already on the calendar. Donald is a three-time Defensive Player of the Year, an eight-time first-team All-Pro, a 10-time Pro Bowl selection, and the 2014 Defensive Rookie of the Year. He has 111 sacks, second among primary defensive tackles since individual sacks became official in 1982, behind John Randle’s 137.5, and he holds the Rams’ franchise record for career sacks. ESPN Research notes that when he appears in a game he will join Deion Sanders as the only players in NFL history to be a five-time first-team All-Pro, have a 750-day layoff, and return. None of that changes the immediate football problem, which is snap count, not résumé. A 35-year-old who has been away for two seasons still has to take double-teams in a team period before anyone stamps him for Melbourne.

The deal itself is a one-year $20 million base with incentives that can push it to $30 million. That is a title-chase rental structure, not a career-cap rewrite. Even before the return, ESPN noted Los Angeles as the Super Bowl LXI favorite at DraftKings, and the only team entering the season with multiple players who had won multiple Defensive Player of the Year awards. The comic is the transaction, not a prediction. Practice-field presence on Sunday is a fact. Game-day presence in Australia is still “one day at a time.” Roster news stops at the contract, the quotes, and the open Week 1 question. It does not invent a snap count that McVay has not posted.

Conclusion

Donald ended the retirement, signed the one-year sheet that can reach $30 million, and was on the grass Sunday after 13 to 14 weeks of grinding. He said he cannot be halfway in and that he is 100% committed. McVay is still deciding whether that ramp is ready for the 49ers in Melbourne. The strip is the hammock folded, the $20 million page with a $30 million star, the gym calendar, and a scoreboard that only reads 100% COMMITTED. The next update is whether he plays Week 1, not whether the résumé was already in Canton conversations. This is the transaction as of Sunday.
