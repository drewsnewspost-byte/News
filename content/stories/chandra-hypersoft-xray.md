What happened

NASA’s Chandra X-ray Observatory archive work, reported via NASA Science, identifies 84 “hypersoft X-ray sources” across six galaxies: the spirals M31 (Andromeda) and M101 (Pinwheel) plus four ellipticals. The sources appear in the lowest-energy X-ray images and vanish in higher-energy bands, while producing intense ultraviolet emission near the soft-X-ray border of the spectrum. The result is published in Nature Astronomy. Ignore wrong galaxy names that may be drawn inside the comic art; the verified set is M31, M101, and four ellipticals.

Astronomers suggest these objects may be binaries powered by black holes, neutron stars, or white dwarfs, and that their ultraviolet output could relate to Type Ia supernova progenitor pathways and to ionization of galactic gas — long-standing open questions the team flags as possible connections, not settled claims.
